## 1. Major groups and their brands

### The Swatch Group, Biel, listed in Switzerland
- Prestige: Breguet, Harry Winston, Blancpain, Glashütte Original, Jaquet Droz.
- High range: Omega, Longines.
- Middle range: Rado, Union Glashütte, Tissot, Certina, Mido, Hamilton, Balmain.
- Basic: Swatch, Flik Flak.
- Industry: ETA movements, Nivarox-FAR hairsprings and escapement parts, Comadur sapphire and ceramics, Universo hands, and dial, case and electronics makers including EM Microelectronic and Micro Crystal.
- Collaborations between Swatch and its own or partner brands, such as the 2022 Omega x Swatch MoonSwatch, have become important volume drivers.
- Swatch Group controls most of the Swiss supply of hairsprings through Nivarox, which gives it influence over the whole Swiss industry.

### Richemont, Geneva, listed in Switzerland
- Watch maisons: A. Lange & Söhne, Cartier, IWC Schaffhausen, Jaeger-LeCoultre, Panerai, Piaget, Roger Dubuis, Vacheron Constantin, plus watches from Van Cleef & Arpels.
- Baume & Mercier was sold to the Damiani Group of Italy; the agreement was announced in January 2026 and completed on 1 July 2026.
- Industrial assets include Manufacture Horlogère ValFleurier and Varinor among others.
- Richemont bought the pre-owned specialist Watchfinder in 2018; check whether it still owns it.

### LVMH, Paris, listed in France
- Watch brands: TAG Heuer, Hublot, Zenith, Bulgari, Louis Vuitton, Tiffany & Co., Chaumet, Dior, and the revived Daniel Roth and Gérald Genta through La Fabrique du Temps Louis Vuitton.

### Seiko Group Corporation, Tokyo
- Seiko, Grand Seiko, Credor, King Seiko as a Seiko line, Lorus, and Pulsar outside Japan.
- Seiko Instruments makes movements, including the TMI line sold to other brands, such as the NH35.
- Orient is a separate company's brand, owned by Seiko Epson.

### Citizen Watch Co., Tokyo
- Citizen, Bulova from 2008, Frédérique Constant, Alpina and Ateliers deMonaco through the 2016 purchase of the Frederique Constant Group, Arnold & Son and Angelus through La Joux-Perret, and the Miyota movement business.
- Citizen's 2012 acquisition of the Prothor holding brought in La Joux-Perret.

### Breitling, Grenchen
- Majority owned by Partners Group since its December 2022 investment, with CVC Capital Partners and management also holding stakes. Partners Group co-founder Alfred Gantner chairs the board, and Georges Kern is CEO.
- Breitling bought Universal Genève in 2023 and Gallet in 2025, and grouped them into a "House of Brands" umbrella. Universal Genève relaunched in 2026 at a price point above Breitling; Gallet is the entry brand.
- Reports in 2026 described writedowns of Breitling's valuation and refinancing pressure at Partners Group. Check current status before relying on it.

### Other groups
- Sowind Group: Girard-Perregaux and Ulysse Nardin, owned by management since a 2022 buyout from Kering.
- Festina Group: Festina, Lotus, Jaguar, Candino, Calypso, and the movement maker Soprod.
- Fossil Group: Fossil, Skagen, Michele, Zodiac, and licensed fashion brands.
- Movado Group: Movado, Ebel, Olivia Burton, MVMT, and licensed fashion brands.
- Timex Group: Timex and a portfolio of licensed brands.
- Sandoz Family Foundation: Parmigiani Fleurier and Vaucher Manufacture Fleurier; Hermès owns a minority stake in Vaucher.
- Damiani Group: Baume & Mercier since 2026, alongside jewelry brands and the Rocca retail chain.
- MELB Holding: H. Moser & Cie, Hautlence, and Precision Engineering AG, a hairspring and component maker.
- Chanel: makes its own watches and holds minority stakes in independents and suppliers, including F.P. Journe, Romain Gauthier, Bell & Ross and Kenissi.

## 2. Large independent and family-owned houses

- Rolex and Tudor: owned by the Hans Wilsdorf Foundation, a private foundation created by the founder. Rolex acquired the Bucherer retail group, announced in August 2023. Bucherer's own watch brand, Carl F. Bucherer, was reported wound down in 2025.
- Patek Philippe: owned by the Stern family since 1932.
- Audemars Piguet: majority owned by the Audemars and Piguet founding families. Holds a stake in Richard Mille and owns the movement maker APRP, Audemars Piguet Renaud & Papi.
- Chopard: owned by the Scheufele family since 1963. Also owns Ferdinand Berthoud as a revived brand.
- Richard Mille: founded 2001; independent with Audemars Piguet as a shareholder.
- Hermès: in-house watch division in Biel.
- Nomos Glashütte, Junghans, Sinn, Stowa, Mühle-Glashütte, Damasko and Laco: German independents of varying scale.
- Oris: independent, family and management owned.
- Longines, Omega and others are group brands; see section 1.

## 3. Notable independents

Independent watchmaking ranges from one-person workshops to companies of a few hundred people. Many are members of the AHCI, the Académie Horlogère des Créateurs Indépendants. Representative names:

- Switzerland: F.P. Journe, Philippe Dufour, Kari Voutilainen, Laurent Ferrier, Greubel Forsey, MB&F, De Bethune, Urwerk, Ressence of Belgium, Czapek, Akrivia and Rexhep Rexhepi, Petermann Bédat, Simon Brette of France working in Geneva, Krayon, Louis Erard, Christophe Claret, Andreas Strehler.
- Germany and Austria: Lang & Heyne, Moritz Grossmann, Habring².
- UK: Roger W. Smith, George Daniels' legacy, Bremont, Christopher Ward, Fears, Garrick.
- Netherlands and Scandinavia: Grönefeld, Christiaan van der Klaauw, Svend Andersen.
- Japan: Masahiro Kikuno, Hajime Asaoka, Kurono Tokyo, Naoya Hida.
- United States: RGM Watch Co., Weiss, Massena LAB, and others.
- Microbrands: a large ecosystem, often using Seiko, Miyota, Sellita or Chinese movements, sold online in small batches. Quality varies widely.

Demand for top independents surged in the 2020s, with auction prices for F.P. Journe and Philippe Dufour rising sharply. In December 2025 an F.P. Journe prototype made for Francis Ford Coppola sold at Phillips for about USD 10.8 million, a record for an independent maker.

History of the ETA supply squeeze: from the early 2000s Swatch Group sought to stop supplying ébauches and movements to competitors. The Swiss competition commission, COMCO, allowed a phased reduction, with the regulated supply regime ending after 2019. This drove the growth of Sellita and in-house programs across the industry.

## 5. Distribution and retail

- Authorized dealers, or ADs, buy from brands and sell with full warranty. For high-demand models, allocation and purchase history can matter.
- Brand boutiques: brands increasingly sell direct through owned or franchised boutiques.
- Major retail groups: Bucherer, owned by Rolex, including the former Tourneau stores in the US; Watches of Switzerland Group in the UK and US; Swiss Watch Gallery, Wempe and others.
- Grey market: new watches sold outside authorized channels, often without the brand's warranty or with a dealer warranty instead.
- Pre-owned and certified pre-owned: Rolex's CPO program began in December 2022 with Bucherer and spread to other Rolex retailers. Other brands run their own programs.
- Online marketplaces: Chrono24, eBay, Watchfinder, WatchBox, Bob's Watches, and forums.
- Auction houses: Phillips, Christie's, Sotheby's, Antiquorum, Bonhams, Monaco Legend Group and others.

## 6. Industry data and economics

- The Federation of the Swiss Watch Industry, FH, publishes monthly Swiss export data by price band, material and destination. It is the most reliable public data for the Swiss industry. Export value is not retail sales.
- Brand-level revenue is mostly private. Morgan Stanley with LuxeConsult publishes widely cited annual estimates, in which Rolex has been estimated at around a third of Swiss watch industry revenue in recent years. Treat these as estimates.
- Listed groups report watch and jewelry segment results: Swatch Group, Richemont, LVMH Watches and Jewelry, Seiko Group, Citizen and Casio.
- Swiss watch exports rose to a record around CHF 26 billion in 2023 and then softened; for any current figure, check FH.
- Unit volumes: Swiss exports are a small fraction of global units by count but dominate by value. China and Hong Kong lead by volume.

## 7. Events, fairs and awards

- Watches and Wonders Geneva: the main annual fair, held each spring since 2022 in person, succeeding the Salon International de la Haute Horlogerie. Rolex, Patek Philippe, Richemont brands and many others launch there.
- Baselworld: the historic Basel fair, which collapsed after 2019 as major brands left.
- Geneva Watch Days, Dubai Watch Week, Time to Watches and other events fill the calendar.
- GPHG, the Grand Prix d'Horlogerie de Genève: annual awards often called the watch industry's Oscars.
- Only Watch: a biennial charity auction for Duchenne muscular dystrophy research in Monaco. It set the all-time wristwatch auction record in 2019 and its 2023 edition was postponed amid controversy; check its current status.

## 8. Trade policy and tariffs

Tariffs are volatile and should always be searched. As of the snapshot:

- In August 2025 the US imposed a 39 percent tariff on most Swiss imports including watches, then agreed in November 2025 to cut it to 15 percent.
- In February 2026 the US Supreme Court was reported to have struck down tariffs imposed under the emergency powers law that had been used. A temporary 10 percent surcharge under another statute followed, and trade press reported it was replaced in July 2026 by a different rate.
- Brands raised US prices several times through 2025 and 2026, citing tariffs and record gold prices.

Pre-owned watches already in a country do not carry fresh import duties when resold there, which is one reason tariffs affect new and pre-owned prices differently.
