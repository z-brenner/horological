# Common Myths and Corrections

Open with the correction, then explain briefly. Avoid lecturing.

## Mechanics and quality

- "More jewels means a better watch." A simple movement is fully jeweled at about 17. Extra jewels usually come from automatic winding or complications, and historically some were decorative. Jewel count is not a quality metric.
- "A higher beat rate always means more accuracy." Higher frequency resists shocks and positional error, but stability, regulation and design matter more. Many 21,600 vph movements are very accurate.
- "In-house movements are always better." In-house often means more exclusivity, sometimes better engineering, but also higher service costs and slower parts availability. A well regulated ETA or Sellita can outperform a mediocre in-house caliber.
- "Tourbillons make wristwatches more accurate." The benefit was for pocket watches held vertically. In a wristwatch, the effect is small and debated. Tourbillons are mainly a display of craft.
- "Automatic watches never need winding." They stop if not worn and may not reach full wind for sedentary wearers. Hand winding helps.
- "You can overwind an automatic." Automatics have slipping bridles. Manual watches have a hard stop, and forcing past it can damage them.
- "Mechanical watches are more accurate than quartz." Quartz is far more accurate. Mechanical watches are valued for craftsmanship, longevity and character.
- "Quartz watches are cheap and low quality." High-end quartz such as Grand Seiko 9F, Citizen 0100 and Breitling SuperQuartz is serious engineering.
- "Spring Drive is a quartz watch." It is powered by a mainspring with a quartz-controlled electromagnetic regulator. It has no battery.
- "A watch that stops needs a new battery or a repair." Mechanical watches stop when unwound; wind it first.

## Water and toughness

- "30 m water resistant means you can dive to 30 m." It means a factory static pressure test equivalent to 30 m; in practice, splash resistance only.
- "Water resistance lasts forever." Gaskets age. Test regularly.
- "A dive watch needs a helium escape valve." Only saturation divers need one.
- "Sapphire crystals are unbreakable." They resist scratches but can chip or shatter under impact.
- "Ceramic bezels last forever." They resist scratches and fading but can crack if struck.

## Origin, branding and history

- "Swiss Made means made entirely in Switzerland." It means at least 60 percent of manufacturing costs, a Swiss movement, casing and final inspection in Switzerland, and technical development in Switzerland.
- "Rolex invented the waterproof watch." Rolex created the first commercially successful waterproof wristwatch with the 1926 Oyster; earlier sealed designs existed.
- "Rolex watches are hand made." Rolex is highly automated, with extensive hand assembly and inspection.
- "Armstrong's Speedmaster was the first watch on the moon." Aldrin wore his outside; Armstrong's stayed in the lunar module.
- "Patek Philippe invented the wristwatch." Breguet made one for Caroline Murat from 1810; Patek made an early Swiss example in 1868, sold in 1876.
- "The Rolex name means 'horological excellence' in French." Wilsdorf said he wanted a short name easy to pronounce in any language and that fit on a dial. Origin stories are anecdotal.
- "Blancpain never made a quartz watch." That was a 1980s marketing line; the brand was dormant through much of the quartz era, and historical details are debated.
- "Old brand founding dates mean continuous history." Many heritage brands were revived trademarks. Check whether production was continuous.

## Value and collecting

- "Watches always appreciate." Most do not. The 2022 peak and correction showed how fragile hype prices are.
- "Box and papers prove authenticity." Papers and boxes are faked and married to the wrong watches.
- "A polished case looks better, so it is worth more." Polishing removes metal and sharp edges, cutting vintage value.
- "Tritium lume is dangerous." Tritium in watch lume is a weak beta emitter and low risk in normal handling. Radium is the real hazard.
- "A tropical dial is damage." Collectors prize even tropical fading. Uneven fading or water damage is different.

## Usage and care

- "Never set the time backward." On most simple watches it is fine. On calendars, chiming and some complications, follow the manual.
- "You should never let an automatic stop." Letting it stop does no harm.
- "Watch winders ruin watches." They add some wear but are not harmful when set correctly.
- "Magnets only matter near MRI machines." Phone cases, laptops, speakers and bag clasps can magnetize watches.
