# Research Playbook

Use this whenever a question goes beyond the reference files, depends on current information, or turns on a detail you cannot confirm. The goal is a sourced answer or an honest account of what remains unknown and how to find it.

## 1. When to search

Search before answering when the question involves:

- Any price: retail, grey market, pre-owned, auction, or service cost.
- Whether a model is current, discontinued, new, or upcoming.
- Who owns a brand, who runs it, or who supplies its movements.
- Specs of a model released or updated after the snapshot date in `SKILL.md`.
- A specific reference, serial, or caliber not covered in the reference files.
- Regulations, tariffs, standards revisions, or warranty terms.
- Auction records and market trends.
- Any fact you would otherwise guess.

## 2. Search method

1. Identify the most specific identifier available: a reference number, caliber number, serial prefix, or exact model name. Specific identifiers beat descriptive phrases.
2. Search the most authoritative source type first, from the map below.
3. Fetch the page rather than relying on snippets for anything that matters.
4. Corroborate any important fact with a second independent source, especially dates, specs and prices.
5. Note publication dates. Watch specs and prices change; an article from 2019 may be wrong today.
6. Cite sources with links in the answer.

## 3. Source map by question type

### Movements and calibers
- Ranfft's watch movement archive, ranfft.de: huge database of historical calibers with photos and specs.
- Caliber Corner, calibercorner.com: modern and vintage caliber specs and which watches use them.
- Watch-Wiki, watch-wiki.org: German-language encyclopedia with caliber and brand pages.
- Manufacturer technical documents: ETA, Sellita, Seiko Instruments, Miyota, and brand technical guides. Seiko and Citizen publish instruction manuals by caliber number on their support sites.

### History, terminology and scholarship
- Fondation de la Haute Horlogerie, hautehorlogerie.org: encyclopedia and glossary.
- NAWCC, nawcc.org: library, bulletin archive and research for watches and clocks.
- British Horological Institute, bhi.co.uk, and the Antiquarian Horological Society, ahsoc.org.
- Europa Star archive, europastar.com: decades of trade reporting.
- Museums: Patek Philippe Museum, Geneva; Musée International d'Horlogerie, La Chaux-de-Fonds; Beyer Clock and Watch Museum, Zurich; Deutsches Uhrenmuseum Glashütte and Furtwangen; Royal Observatory Greenwich; British Museum horology collection; Seiko Museum Ginza; Smithsonian.
- Books: George Daniels, "Watchmaking"; Donald de Carle, "Practical Watch Repairing"; "The Theory of Horology" from the Swiss technical schools; Dava Sobel, "Longitude," for a popular account of Harrison with known simplifications; brand monographs and reference books for Rolex, Omega, Patek Philippe and Heuer.

### Specific brands and references
- Brand heritage pages and press archives.
- Omega's vintage database on omegawatches.com.
- Rolex: Jake's Rolex World, Rolex Passion Report, and Rolex Forums.
- Heuer: OnTheDash.
- Seiko: Plus9Time, The Watch Site, the Seiko and Citizen Watch Forum, and Seiko's own caliber manuals.
- Grand Seiko, Patek, AP and others: brand sites plus specialist forums such as PuristSPro and WatchProSite.
- Watchuseek and Omega Forums for practical ownership knowledge across brands.

### Obscure names and trademarks
- Mikrolisk, mikrolisk.de: identifies watch brand names, trademarks and logos with their registrants.
- Swiss trademark register Swissreg and WIPO's Global Brand Database for trademark history.

### Patents
- Google Patents and Espacenet. Search by inventor, assignee and keywords; Swiss patents are CH-prefixed.

### Market and prices
- Auction results: phillips.com, christies.com, sothebys.com, antiquorum.swiss, monacolegendauctions.com, bonhams.com.
- WatchCharts: indices and model price histories.
- Chrono24: asking prices and price history.
- eBay sold listings: transaction prices.
- Dealers' "sold" archives.
- Industry data: Federation of the Swiss Watch Industry, fhs.swiss, for monthly exports; Morgan Stanley and LuxeConsult annual brand estimates as reported in the press.

### News and reviews
- Original reporting and analysis: Watches by SJX, Hodinkee, Quill & Pad, Revolution, Europa Star, WatchTime, Monochrome, Fratello, Worn & Wound, A Blog to Watch, Time and Tide, and Swiss business press such as NZZ, Le Temps and Swissinfo.
- Business news: Bloomberg, Reuters, Financial Times for group results, ownership and tariffs.

### Standards and regulation
- ISO catalog pages, iso.org, give the scope and edition of standards; full text is paid.
- COSC, cosc.swiss; METAS, metas.ch; the Geneva Seal office; the Fleurier Quality Foundation.
- The Swiss ordinance on "Swiss" for watches, and FH guidance on Swiss Made.

### Servicing and technical repair
- Brand service manuals and technical communications, where available.
- Watchmaker supply houses such as Cousins UK, Esslinger and Otto Frei for parts identification and lubricant charts.
- Watchmaker forums and NAWCC message boards.

## 4. Query patterns that work

- Reference numbers in quotes: "ref 5513" "1675" "145.022".
- Caliber plus spec: "caliber 3235 power reserve", "NH35 specifications".
- Caliber plus problem: "7750 low amplitude", "6R35 hacking".
- Forum-restricted: site:omegaforums.net 105.012 dial variations; site:rolexforums.com 1016 serial.
- Primary-source hunting: "patent" plus mechanism and maker, "archive extract" plus brand.
- Auction hunting: reference plus "lot" plus house name, or "sold" plus year.
- Date-bounded searches for anything time-sensitive: include the current year.
- Non-English sources for regional brands: German for Glashütte and German brands, Japanese for Seiko domestic models, French for Swiss industry news, Russian for Soviet watches.

## 5. Source reliability tiers

1. Primary sources: patents, brand archives and extracts, original catalogs and price lists, official standards, legal texts, auction catalogs with condition reports.
2. Expert secondary sources: specialist books, long-running reference sites, auction house essays, museums, and journalists with documented original reporting.
3. Editorial outlets and reviews: valuable, but often repeat brand press releases. Prefer articles with hands-on inspection and named sources.
4. Forums: excellent for owner experience and obscure variants; uneven on history and specs. Look for consensus among known experts and photographic evidence.
5. Brand marketing: a source of claims, not proof. Brand-written history often omits failures and inflates firsts.
6. Low-quality web content: SEO listicles, AI-generated pages and content farms. Do not rely on them.

## 6. Traps

- Circular sourcing: many articles copy the same unsourced claim. Five sources repeating one error are still one source.
- Marketing firsts: "first," "only" and "most" claims are often narrowly defined.
- Stale specs: brands update calibers mid-production without changing model names.
- Regional variation: model numbers, prices and specs differ by market.
- Asking prices presented as values.
- Survivorship bias in auction coverage: records get headlines, unsold lots and losses do not.
- AI-generated misinformation: fabricated reference numbers and specs appear on low-quality sites. Verify against tier 1 or 2 sources.
- Search results dated in the future or with inconsistent dates: check the date on the page itself.

## 7. When sources conflict

1. State the conflict and the competing claims.
2. Weigh by tier, recency, independence and specificity.
3. Say which you find more convincing and why.
4. Name what would settle it: a patent, archive extract, catalog, movement photo, or brand confirmation.

## 8. When nothing settles it

- Say plainly what is known, what is not, and your best hypothesis with its confidence.
- Give the single fastest route to an answer: the brand heritage department, a specialist forum with the right photos, an auction specialist, an independent watchmaker, or a museum curator.
- Never invent reference numbers, dates, prices or specifications to fill the gap.
