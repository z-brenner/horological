# Caliber Reference

Specifications are typical published or widely cited figures. Brands sometimes modify a base caliber, changing power reserve, jewel count or regulation grade, so confirm the exact version for a specific watch. For anything not listed, use Ranfft, Caliber Corner or the maker's technical sheet; see `research-playbook.md`.

Abbreviations: PR power reserve, J jewels, H hacking, HW hand-winding.

| Brand caliber | Base |
|---|---|
| Omega 1120 | ETA 2892-A2 |
| Breitling B17 | ETA 2824-2 |
| Breitling B13 | Valjoux 7750 |
| TAG Heuer Calibre 5 | ETA 2824-2 or Sellita SW200 |
| TAG Heuer Calibre 16 | Valjoux 7750 or Sellita SW500 |
| Cartier 049 | ETA 2892-A2 |
| IWC 30110 | ETA 2892-A2 or Sellita SW300 |
| Oris 733 | Sellita SW200-1 |
| Hamilton H-10 | ETA C07 Powermatic 80 family |
| Longines L888 | ETA A31.L11, a long-reserve ETA design |
| Panerai OP calibers of the 1990s and 2000s | Unitas 6497 and 6498, ETA 2824 and Valjoux 7750 |

Rebadging is normal, legal and not a flaw. It matters for servicing, parts cost, and judging value.

## 11. Clones and equivalents

- After ETA patents expired, Sellita made functional equivalents. Many parts interchange, but not all; watchmakers check.
- Chinese clones of ETA, Unitas and Valjoux designs vary from decent to poor. Clones also appear in counterfeits. A caliber that looks like an ETA but has a different finishing pattern, jewel count, or engraving may indicate a fake or a clone.
- Japanese movements are sometimes used where a Swiss one is expected, which may break Swiss Made rules.
